content_and_progress: '<p><b style= "color: #26bde2; font-size: large">6.1.b Nitrate in groundwater</b><br><br>The nitrate content of groundwater is monitored by the Bundesländer as part of the reporting on groundwater status in Germany to the European Environment Agency (<abbr title="European Environment Agency" tabindex="0">EEA</abbr>). The monitoring sites used for this purpose constitute the so-called <abbr title="European Environment Agency" tabindex="0">EEA</abbr> monitoring network. This network comprises 1,214&nbsp;sampling points and provides a representative coverage of Germany. The data is compiled by the German Environment Agency (Umweltbundesamt) based on information provided by the Federal/Länder Working Group on Water (<abbr title="German Working Group on Water Issues of the Länder and the Federal Government" tabindex="0">LAWA</abbr>).<br><br>As with the indicator for phosphorus concentrations in rivers, this indicator does not provide information on the extent to which threshold values are exceeded or undershot. It measures the proportion of all sampling sites at which the specified threshold value is met. Nitrate levels may have decreased significantly at certain sites; however, as long as concentrations remain above the threshold of 50&nbsp;milligrams per litre, such improvements are not reflected in the indicator. Similarly, increasing nitrate levels that remain below the threshold are also not captured. It must also be considered that measures to reduce nitrate pollution may only take effect with a delay, as the percolation time from the surface to the groundwater can span several years.<br><br>Naturally occurring nitrate levels range from 0&nbsp;to a maximum of 10&nbsp;milligrams per litre. Concentrations between 10&nbsp;and 25&nbsp;mg/L indicate low to moderate contamination, while levels between 25&nbsp;and 50&nbsp;mg/L indicate significant groundwater pollution. Exceeding the threshold of 50&nbsp;mg/L as defined by the German Groundwater Ordinance&nbsp;–&nbsp;on which this indicator is based&nbsp;–&nbsp;means the groundwater is in poor chemical condition and cannot be used as drinking water without treatment.<br><br>In 2020, the nitrate concentration remained below the threshold of 50&nbsp;mg/L at 84.1% of all monitoring stations. Since 2008, the proportion of sites complying with this threshold has remained nearly unchanged. As a result, the goal of full compliance across all monitoring points has not been achieved, and no discernible trend in that direction has emerged. Conversely, in 2020, the threshold was exceeded at 15.9% of the groundwater monitoring sites in the <abbr title="European Environment Agency" tabindex="0">EEA</abbr> network. At 17.8% of the sites, nitrate levels ranged between 25&nbsp;and 50&nbsp;mg/L&nbsp;–&nbsp;still indicating elevated pollution. This share has also remained largely constant over the years.<br><br>Groundwater nitrate pollution is primarily caused by the leaching of nitrate from various nitrogen-based fertilisers. These include organic fertilisers such as slurry and manure, as well as mineral fertilisers used in intensive crop production. In recent years, digestate&nbsp;–&nbsp;a by-product of biogas production&nbsp;–&nbsp;has increasingly been applied as fertiliser in agriculture. When fertilisation is not aligned with plant nutrient demand, these inputs can significantly contribute to elevated nitrate concentrations in groundwater. Therefore, the development of Indicator 2.1.a "Nitrogen Surplus in Agriculture" has a direct influence on groundwater nitrate contamination.<br><br>To assess the specific impact of agricultural land use on nitrate pollution in water bodies, a separate nitrate report is submitted to the European Union (<abbr title="European Union" tabindex="0">EU</abbr>). For this purpose, a subset of monitoring stations from the <abbr title="European Environment Agency" tabindex="0">EEA</abbr> network is selected, representing catchment areas dominated by agricultural use. As expected, nitrate pollution in this specialised monitoring network is above the average level captured by Indicator 6.1.b.</p>'
